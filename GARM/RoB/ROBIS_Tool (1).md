# ROBIS Tool (Risk Of Bias In Systematic reviews)

## Overview
The ROBIS tool is designed to assess the risk of bias in systematic reviews. Developed by Whiting et al. (2016), it provides a structured approach to evaluate whether the methods, conduct, and reporting of a review might introduce bias into its findings. ROBIS is particularly useful for systematic review of interventions but can be applied to other types of reviews.

## Purpose and Application
ROBIS helps users to:
- Assess the reliability of systematic review findings
- Identify potential methodological weaknesses that might have introduced bias
- Guide the conduct of new systematic reviews
- Inform decision-making based on the results of systematic reviews

## Three Phases of ROBIS

### Phase 1: Assessing Relevance (Optional)
This optional phase helps users determine whether the systematic review being assessed is relevant to their question of interest.

#### Key Considerations:
- Does the review question match the question of interest for your assessment?
- Are the PICO elements (Population, Intervention, Comparator, Outcome) of the review aligned with your assessment objectives?
- Is the context (e.g., settings, time frames) appropriate for your purpose?

#### Process:
1. Specify your question of interest
2. Specify the PICO elements of the review being assessed
3. Judge whether the review is relevant to your question
4. Decide whether to proceed with the full ROBIS assessment

#### Example Assessment:
- **Your question**: Effectiveness of cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) for depression in adults
- **Review question**: Effectiveness of psychotherapies for depression in adults
- **Judgment**: Partially relevant (broader intervention scope but otherwise aligned)

### Phase 2: Identifying Concerns with the Review Process
This phase evaluates four key domains of the systematic review process to identify potential methodological concerns.

#### Domain 1: Study Eligibility Criteria

##### Signaling Questions:
1.1 Did the review adhere to pre-defined objectives and eligibility criteria?
1.2 Were the eligibility criteria appropriate for the review question?
1.3 Were eligibility criteria unambiguous?
1.4 Were all restrictions in eligibility criteria based on study characteristics appropriate?
1.5 Were any restrictions in eligibility criteria based on sources of information appropriate?

##### Examples:
- **Low concern**: Clear pre-specified criteria aligned with review question; appropriate inclusion/exclusion criteria; no unjustified restrictions
- **High concern**: Post-hoc changes to eligibility criteria; exclusion of studies based on language without justification; overly restrictive criteria
- **Unclear concern**: Insufficient information about pre-specification of criteria; ambiguous description of eligibility criteria

##### Guidance for Assessment:
- Check if a protocol was registered or published before the review was conducted
- Evaluate whether the criteria allow for inclusion of all relevant studies
- Consider if any restrictions (e.g., language, publication status) were justified
- Assess if the criteria were specific enough to enable consistent application

#### Domain 2: Identification and Selection of Studies

##### Signaling Questions:
2.1 Did the search include an appropriate range of databases/electronic sources for published and unpublished reports?
2.2 Were methods additional to database searching used to identify relevant reports?
2.3 Were the terms and structure of the search strategy likely to retrieve as many eligible studies as possible?
2.4 Were restrictions based on date, publication format, or language appropriate?
2.5 Were efforts made to minimize error in selection of studies?

##### Examples:
- **Low concern**: Comprehensive search across multiple databases; additional search methods used; no unjustified restrictions; duplicate screening
- **High concern**: Search limited to a single database; no searching of trial registries for recent studies; inappropriate date restrictions
- **Unclear concern**: Insufficient detail about search strategy; unclear if screening was done by multiple reviewers

##### Guidance for Assessment:
- Check if multiple relevant databases were searched
- Evaluate if additional methods were used (e.g., reference checking, contacting experts)
- Assess if search terms were appropriate and comprehensive
- Consider if study selection was performed by at least two reviewers independently

#### Domain 3: Data Collection and Study Appraisal

##### Signaling Questions:
3.1 Were efforts made to minimize error in data collection?
3.2 Were sufficient study characteristics available for both review authors and readers to be able to interpret the results?
3.3 Were all relevant study results collected for use in the synthesis?
3.4 Was risk of bias (or methodological quality) formally assessed using appropriate criteria?
3.5 Were efforts made to minimize error in risk of bias assessment?

##### Examples:
- **Low concern**: Data extraction by two independent reviewers; comprehensive collection of study characteristics; appropriate risk of bias assessment
- **High concern**: Single reviewer data extraction without verification; inadequate reporting of study characteristics; no risk of bias assessment
- **Unclear concern**: Unclear process for data extraction; limited reporting of study characteristics

##### Guidance for Assessment:
- Check if data extraction was performed by at least two reviewers independently
- Evaluate if sufficient information about included studies was reported
- Assess if an appropriate tool was used for risk of bias assessment
- Consider if risk of bias assessment was performed by at least two reviewers independently

#### Domain 4: Synthesis and Findings

##### Signaling Questions:
4.1 Did the synthesis include all studies that it should?
4.2 Were all pre-defined analyses reported or departures explained?
4.3 Was the synthesis appropriate given the nature and similarity in the research questions, study designs and outcomes across included studies?
4.4 Was between-study variation (heterogeneity) minimal or addressed in the synthesis?
4.5 Were the findings robust, e.g., as demonstrated through funnel plot or sensitivity analyses?
4.6 Were biases in primary studies minimal or addressed in the synthesis?

##### Examples:
- **Low concern**: All eligible studies included in synthesis; appropriate synthesis methods; heterogeneity addressed; sensitivity analyses conducted
- **High concern**: Eligible studies excluded from synthesis without justification; inappropriate pooling of dissimilar studies; heterogeneity ignored
- **Unclear concern**: Unclear if all eligible studies were included; limited information about synthesis methods

##### Guidance for Assessment:
- Check if all eligible studies were included in the synthesis
- Evaluate if the synthesis methods were appropriate for the available data
- Assess if heterogeneity was appropriately investigated and addressed
- Consider if potential biases in primary studies were considered in the synthesis

### Phase 3: Judging Risk of Bias

This phase involves making an overall judgment about the risk of bias in the review findings.

#### Process:
1. Summarize concerns identified in Phase 2 across all four domains
2. Consider whether identified concerns are likely to affect the review findings
3. Judge whether the review is at low, high, or unclear risk of bias

#### Key Questions for Overall Judgment:
1. Did the interpretation of findings address all concerns identified in Domains 1-4?
2. Was the relevance of identified studies to the review's research question appropriately considered?
3. Did the reviewers avoid emphasizing results based on statistical significance?
4. Were the review authors' conclusions supported by the available data?

#### Examples:
- **Low risk of bias**: Minor concerns across domains that are unlikely to affect review findings; appropriate interpretation of results
- **High risk of bias**: Major concerns in one or more domains that are likely to affect review findings; interpretation does not consider limitations
- **Unclear risk of bias**: Insufficient information to determine the impact of potential biases on review findings

## Practical Application

### Step-by-Step Guide
1. **Prepare for assessment**:
   - Gather all relevant information about the review (full text, protocol, supplementary materials)
   - Familiarize yourself with the ROBIS tool and guidance
   
2. **Complete Phase 1 (optional)**:
   - Determine if the review is relevant to your question of interest
   
3. **Complete Phase 2**:
   - For each domain, answer all signaling questions
   - Provide justification for each answer
   - Make a judgment about level of concern for each domain (low, high, unclear)
   
4. **Complete Phase 3**:
   - Summarize concerns from Phase 2
   - Answer the key questions for overall judgment
   - Make an overall judgment about risk of bias (low, high, unclear)
   
5. **Document the assessment**:
   - Record all judgments and justifications
   - Summarize the key limitations of the review

### Implementation Tips
- Assessments should be made by at least two independent reviewers
- Disagreements should be resolved through discussion or by involving a third reviewer
- Detailed justifications should be provided for all judgments
- Consider the impact of identified concerns on the specific review findings of interest

## Example Assessment

### Review: Smith et al. (2022) - A systematic review of cognitive behavioral therapy for anxiety disorders

#### Phase 1: Relevance
- **Your question**: Effectiveness of CBT for generalized anxiety disorder
- **Review question**: Effectiveness of CBT for all anxiety disorders
- **Judgment**: Relevant (broader but includes population of interest)

#### Phase 2: Domain Assessments

##### Domain 1: Study Eligibility Criteria
- 1.1 Pre-defined objectives and criteria? **Yes** - Protocol registered in PROSPERO
- 1.2 Appropriate eligibility criteria? **Yes** - Criteria aligned with review question
- 1.3 Unambiguous criteria? **Probably Yes** - Clear definitions provided
- 1.4 Appropriate restrictions on study characteristics? **Yes** - Only RCTs included, which is appropriate
- 1.5 Appropriate restrictions on information sources? **Probably No** - Excluded non-English studies without justification
- **Judgment**: Some concerns

##### Domain 2: Identification and Selection of Studies
- 2.1 Appropriate range of databases? **Yes** - Multiple relevant databases searched
- 2.2 Additional methods used? **Yes** - Reference checking and expert consultation
- 2.3 Appropriate search strategy? **Probably Yes** - Comprehensive terms used
- 2.4 Appropriate restrictions? **Probably No** - Limited to last 10 years without clear justification
- 2.5 Efforts to minimize error in selection? **Yes** - Duplicate screening performed
- **Judgment**: Some concerns

##### Domain 3: Data Collection and Study Appraisal
- 3.1 Efforts to minimize error in data collection? **Yes** - Duplicate data extraction
- 3.2 Sufficient study characteristics available? **Yes** - Comprehensive characteristics reported
- 3.3 All relevant results collected? **Probably Yes** - All specified outcomes extracted
- 3.4 Risk of bias formally assessed? **Yes** - Cochrane RoB tool used
- 3.5 Efforts to minimize error in risk of bias assessment? **Yes** - Independent assessments by two reviewers
- **Judgment**: Low concerns

##### Domain 4: Synthesis and Findings
- 4.1 Synthesis included all studies? **Yes** - All eligible studies included
- 4.2 All pre-defined analyses reported? **Probably Yes** - Analyses followed protocol
- 4.3 Synthesis appropriate? **Yes** - Meta-analysis appropriate for included studies
- 4.4 Heterogeneity addressed? **Yes** - Subgroup and sensitivity analyses conducted
- 4.5 Findings robust? **Probably Yes** - Sensitivity analyses supported main findings
- 4.6 Biases in primary studies addressed? **Probably Yes** - Risk of bias considered in interpretation
- **Judgment**: Low concerns

#### Phase 3: Risk of Bias
- Interpretation addressed concerns? **Probably Yes** - Limitations discussed
- Relevance of studies considered? **Yes** - Applicability of findings discussed
- Avoided emphasis on statistical significance? **Probably Yes** - Clinical significance also considered
- Conclusions supported by data? **Yes** - Conclusions appropriately cautious
- **Overall judgment**: Low risk of bias
