# ROBINS-I Tool (Risk Of Bias In Non-randomized Studies of Interventions)

## Overview
The ROBINS-I tool is designed to evaluate the risk of bias in non-randomized studies of interventions (NRSI). It provides a systematic approach to assess how closely a non-randomized study approximates the results of a hypothetical randomized trial.

## Seven Domains of Bias

### 1. Bias Due to Confounding
- Assesses whether all important confounding domains were appropriately controlled for
- Evaluates whether confounding was measured and controlled for appropriately
- Considers post-intervention variables that could be affected by the intervention

### 2. Bias in Selection of Participants
- Evaluates if selection into the study was related to intervention and outcome
- Assesses if start of follow-up and start of intervention coincide for most participants
- Examines adjustments for selection biases

### 3. Bias in Classification of Interventions
- Assesses if intervention groups were clearly defined
- Evaluates if intervention status was determined and defined retrospectively
- Considers if intervention definition was based on information collected at the time of intervention

### 4. Bias Due to Deviations from Intended Interventions
- Evaluates if there were deviations from the intended intervention
- Assesses if these deviations were balanced between groups
- Considers if appropriate analysis was used to estimate the effect of starting and adhering to intervention

### 5. Bias Due to Missing Data
- Evaluates data completeness for intervention and outcome
- Assesses if participants were excluded due to missing data
- Considers if missing data could depend on true values

### 6. Bias in Measurement of Outcomes
- Examines if outcome assessors were aware of intervention status
- Evaluates if outcome measurement could be influenced by knowledge of intervention
- Considers if outcome assessment methods differed between intervention groups

### 7. Bias in Selection of the Reported Result
- Assesses if data were analyzed in accordance with a pre-specified plan
- Evaluates if multiple outcome measurements or analyses were reported selectively

## Signaling Questions
Each domain contains specific signaling questions that guide assessors in determining the risk of bias. These questions require "Yes," "Probably yes," "Probably no," "No," or "No information" responses.

## Risk of Bias Judgment
For each domain and overall, the tool generates a judgment:
- **Low risk of bias**: The study is comparable to a well-performed randomized trial
- **Moderate risk of bias**: The study is sound for a non-randomized study but not comparable to a well-performed randomized trial
- **Serious risk of bias**: The study has important problems
- **Critical risk of bias**: The study is too problematic to provide any useful evidence
- **No information**: No information on which to base a judgment

## Application Process
1. Specify the review question and target trial
2. Specify the outcome and effect of interest
3. Answer signaling questions for each domain
4. Reach domain-level judgments
5. Reach an overall judgment

## Implementation
The assessment is typically conducted by two independent reviewers, with disagreements resolved through discussion or by a third reviewer.
