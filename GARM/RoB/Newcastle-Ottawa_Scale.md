# Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (NOS)

## Overview
The Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (NOS) is a quality assessment tool designed for evaluating non-randomized studies, particularly cohort and case-control studies in systematic reviews and meta-analyses. It was developed by the Ottawa Hospital Research Institute to assess the quality of observational studies.

## Structure
The NOS evaluates studies from three broad perspectives:

### 1. Selection of Study Groups
- For cohort studies: representativeness of the exposed cohort, selection of non-exposed cohort, ascertainment of exposure, demonstration that outcome of interest was not present at start of study
- For case-control studies: adequacy of case definition, representativeness of cases, selection of controls, definition of controls

### 2. Comparability of Groups
- Comparability of cohorts/cases and controls on the basis of design or analysis

### 3. Ascertainment of Exposure/Outcome
- For cohort studies: assessment of outcome, adequacy of follow-up length, adequacy of follow-up of cohorts
- For case-control studies: ascertainment of exposure, same method of ascertainment for cases and controls, non-response rate

## Star System
The NOS uses a star system for semi-quantitative assessment of study quality:
- A maximum of one star for each item within the Selection and Outcome/Exposure categories
- A maximum of two stars for Comparability
- Total maximum score is 9 stars (highest quality)

### Score Interpretation
- High-quality studies: 7-9 stars
- Medium-quality studies: 4-6 stars
- Low-quality studies: 0-3 stars
(Note: These cutoffs may vary between reviews)

## Application
- Two separate scales are available for cohort and case-control studies
- Each study is evaluated independently by at least two reviewers
- Disagreements are resolved through discussion or by a third reviewer

## Advantages
- Simple and easy to use
- Widely accepted in the scientific community
- Addresses key domains of bias in observational studies
- Facilitates transparent reporting of study quality

## Limitations
- Subjective interpretation of some items
- Limited guidance for scoring some items
- Does not address all potential sources of bias
- May not be suitable for all types of observational studies

## Usage in Systematic Reviews
The NOS is commonly used to:
- Exclude low-quality studies from meta-analyses
- Perform sensitivity analyses based on study quality
- Weight studies according to their methodological quality
- Explore heterogeneity between studies
